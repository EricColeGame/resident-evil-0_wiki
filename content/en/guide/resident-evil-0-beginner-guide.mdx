Resident Evil 0 is the odd one out in the classic survival horror lineup — and this Resident Evil 0 beginner guide exists because of one uncomfortable truth: the game punishes habits you built in every other Resident Evil title. There are no item boxes to stash your loot in, no way to leave your partner behind, and you will haul a grappling hook around far longer than you'd like.

None of that is a design flaw. It's the point. A good Resident Evil 0 beginner guide teaches you to work with those rules instead of fighting them, and once they click, the prequel becomes one of the most satisfying entries in the series.

## Why Resident Evil 0 Feels Harder Than It Looks

Resident Evil 0 launched on the Nintendo GameCube in 2002 as a direct prequel to the original game, and it was later remastered for modern consoles and PC. It runs on the same design philosophy as the 2002 remake of the first game: fixed camera angles, tank controls, and typewriters instead of autosaves.

What separates it is structure. You control two characters at once, and the game removes centralized item storage entirely. Every decision about what to carry, what to drop, and who holds it has consequences that ripple forward for hours — sometimes across an entire chapter.

| System | RE1 / RE2 era | Resident Evil 0 |
|---|---|---|
| Item storage | Item boxes in save rooms | Drop items anywhere; they stay put |
| Party | One hero | Two heroes, swap at will |
| Inventory | One 6–8 slot bag | Two 6-slot inventories |
| Herb mixing | Any character | Rebecca only |
| Heavy lifting | Rarely relevant | Billy only |
| Saving | Typewriter + ink ribbon | Typewriter + ink ribbon |

## The Partner Swap System: Your Most Important Button

Every Resident Evil 0 beginner guide should start here, because swapping is the game's real difficulty curve. A single button hands control from Rebecca Chambers to Billy Coen or back again, and the inactive character becomes an AI partner who follows you and fights on their own.

You can't micromanage that AI with a deep command menu. What you *can* control is positioning, loadout, and timing. When both characters stand in the same room, you can also exchange items directly — faster and safer than dumping things on the floor and picking them back up.

| Trait | Rebecca Chambers | Billy Coen |
|---|---|---|
| Role | S.T.A.R.S. Bravo Team medic | Escaped convict, ex-Marine |
| Inventory slots | 6 | 6 |
| Herb mixing | Yes — she's the only one who can | No |
| Heavy objects | No | Yes — crates, statues, machinery |
| Durability | Lower | Noticeably higher |
| Weapon notes | Cannot use the shotgun | The shotgun is his to carry |

Practical swap habits that pay off immediately:

- **Lead with Billy in dangerous rooms.** He soaks more damage and pushes the heavy props you'll need for puzzles.
- **Swap back to Rebecca for item and puzzle work.** She mixes herbs and handles the fiddly key-item routing.
- **Never swap mid-fight unless you have to.** The transition costs you a beat, and zombies don't wait.
- **Keep both characters armed.** If a scripted moment separates them, an unarmed partner is a dead partner.

## Item Management Without Item Boxes

This is where a Resident Evil 0 beginner guide earns its keep. Since dropped items persist permanently on the floor, the entire map effectively becomes your storage chest — but only if you're organized about it.

Community reports and long-running player experience agree on one thing: the Hookshot is the most hated item in the game. It's a key item you'll need in more than one location, and it eats inventory space the whole time you're carrying it.

| Item type | Best carrier | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Handguns + ammo | Both | Both characters can fire handguns |
| Shotgun + shells | Billy | Rebecca can't use it |
| Grenade launcher / magnum | Rebecca | Keeps Billy free for shotgun duty |
| Mixed herbs | Rebecca | She's the one who mixes them |
| Key and puzzle items | Whoever is heading there | Avoid double-carrying duplicates |
| Hookshot | The character you'll use it with | Never split it from its owner |

A few rules of thumb:

- Create **staging rooms** — safe, central areas where you dump spare ammo, herbs, and backup weapons you'll swing back through later.
- Never drop a key item in a room you can't revisit.
- Leave health with the character who's more likely to get grabbed.
- Before a boss door, consolidate. Empty pockets and full hands beat a perfect inventory you left three rooms ago.

## Ammo, Healing, and the Herb Economy

Resident Evil 0 is stingy with ammo, and Rebecca's herb mixing is your main lever. Red herbs don't heal on their own — they amplify whatever you combine them with, so treat them as multipliers rather than medicine.

| Mix | Approximate effect | When to use it |
|---|---|---|
| Single green | Small recovery | Topping off between fights |
| Green + green | Moderate recovery | After a rough encounter |
| Green + green + green | Strong recovery | When red herbs are scarce |
| Green + red | Full recovery | Right before a boss |
| Red alone | No healing | Don't waste the slot |

Pair that with smart target selection:

| Enemy | Threat level | Best response |
|---|---|---|
| Zombie | Low to moderate | Walk past, or one clean headshot |
| Infected dog | Moderate | Kill quickly; they ambush in packs |
| Leech swarm | High (ammo sink) | Run. They reform and drain your ammo |
| Hunter | Very high | Shotgun or grenade rounds; never let it close |
| Eliminator (infected monkey) | High | Heavy weapons; it dodges handgun fire |
| Proto Tyrant | Boss | Keep distance, save the magnum |

Two more habits worth building early: don't shoot enemies you can simply walk around, and don't burn handgun ammo on anything that shrugs it off.

## A Beginner-Friendly Progression Roadmap

The game moves through three broad phases. Knowing what each one demands keeps you from over-preparing for the wrong problems.

| Stage | Setting | Beginner focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ecliptic Express (the train) | Learn swapping, grab everything, don't hoard ammo |
| 2 | Umbrella Management Training Facility | Central hub; batch your backtracking, manage the Hookshot |
| 3 | Underground lab and treatment plant | Heavier enemies; save magnum and grenade rounds |

**Stage 1** is the tutorial in disguise. Practice switching characters under pressure and get comfortable dropping items in the corridors you'll re-cross.

**Stage 2** is where most players stall. The facility is a hub with spokes, so plan routes that accomplish two or three objectives per trip rather than one.

**Stage 3** punishes panic. Keep your heaviest ammunition for the final stretch, and make sure Rebecca is holding the mixed herbs when the last fights begin.

## Five Mistakes New Players Make

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Hoarding everything | Both inventories fill fast | Use staging rooms instead of carrying it all |
| Ignoring Billy's strength | Rebecca takes heavy damage | Front-load risky rooms with Billy |
| Fighting every enemy | Ammo dries up before the finale | Dodge what you can, kill what blocks you |
| Forgetting the Hookshot | Forced backtracking | Track where you left it, always |
| Saving carelessly | Ink ribbons are limited on standard difficulties | Save before bosses, not after every room |

A final note on difficulty: if you're completely new to tank controls, there's no shame in starting on the easiest setting. Resident Evil 0's reputation for difficulty comes mostly from inventory pressure, and learning the map first makes a harder run far more enjoyable later.

## FAQ

**Does this Resident Evil 0 beginner guide apply to the HD remaster?**
Yes. The remaster keeps the partner swap system, the lack of item boxes, and the herb mixing rules intact. Only the visuals and platform options changed.

**Should I play Resident Evil 0 before the original game?**
It's a prequel, so the story works either way. That said, playing the first game first makes the controls and save system feel less overwhelming, because Resident Evil 0 layers a second character on top of them.

**Can I avoid the Hookshot entirely?**
No — it's required for progression in more than one area. The best you can do is keep it with the character who needs it next so you're not ferrying it back and forth.

**Is there anything to unlock after finishing?**
Yes. Completing the main campaign unlocks a bonus mode that rewards thorough exploration, which is a good reason to sweep rooms properly on your first run rather than sprinting to the credits.
