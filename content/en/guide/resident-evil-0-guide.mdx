Resident Evil 0 is the black sheep of the classic series — a prequel that strands you on a zombie-infested train with two playable characters, no item boxes, and a partner who will absolutely die if you forget about them. That is exactly why this Resident Evil 0 guide exists: the game punishes habits you built in Resident Evil 1, 2, and 3, and the Partner Zapping System rewires how you plan every single room. Whether you are playing the original GameCube release or the HD Remaster on modern platforms, the fundamentals below will keep your inventory clean, your ammo budget honest, and both survivors breathing.

## Resident Evil 0 at a Glance

Before diving into tactics, it helps to know what kind of game you are actually playing. Resident Evil 0 is a fixed-camera survival horror title set in July 1998, hours before the Mansion Incident that kicks off the first Resident Evil.

| Detail | What You Need to Know |
|---|---|
| Original release | 2002, Nintendo GameCube |
| Modern version | Resident Evil 0 HD Remaster, available on current platforms and PC |
| Timeline | Set in July 1998, immediately before the original Resident Evil |
| Protagonists | Rebecca Chambers (S.T.A.R.S. Bravo Team medic) and Billy Coen (escaped convict, ex-Marine) |
| Main locations | Ecliptic Express train, Umbrella's management training facility, underground laboratory, treatment plant |
| Signature mechanic | Partner Zapping System — swap between characters on the fly |
| Biggest departure | No item boxes; anything you drop stays exactly where you left it |
| Saving | Typewriter plus ink ribbons, which are a limited resource |

If you have only played the remakes, expect tank controls, deliberate pacing, and resource pressure. If you have played the originals, expect all of that with one extra headache: a second character you have to equip, position, and occasionally rescue.

## Master the Partner Zapping System

The Partner Zapping System is the mechanic that defines Resident Evil 0. At almost any moment outside of scripted scenes, you can swap control between Rebecca and Billy. The character you are not controlling follows along and can take damage, use up nothing, and generally act as a moving obstacle — or a spare set of pockets.

Swapping is not just a novelty. It is how you solve the game efficiently, because the two characters have genuinely different strengths.

### Rebecca vs. Billy: Who Does What

| Trait | Rebecca Chambers | Billy Coen |
|---|---|---|
| Max health | Lower | Higher |
| Herb mixing | Yes — she handles the herb combinations | No — hand him finished healing items |
| Starting gear | Handgun, knife | Knife |
| Best combat role | Ranged support, ammo management | Front line, absorbing hits |
| Best utility role | Chemistry puzzles, healing stock | Carrying heavy weapon loadouts |
| Story function | Bravo Team medic, lab work | Ex-Marine with combat experience |

**Practical rule:** Rebecca mixes. Billy carries. In most successful runs, Rebecca turns loose herbs into strong healing items while Billy hauls the shotgun, the heavy ammo, and anything else that eats inventory space.

### When to Swap (and When Not To)

- **Swap before a door, not after.** If a room has a scripted event, you want the right character standing there first.
- **Leave the partner behind cover.** A follower standing in a zombie's path is free damage against you.
- **Do not swap mid-fight** unless you need the other character's weapon. The animation and the AI reset cost you precious seconds.
- **Check both inventories before a boss room.** Player experience consistently shows that the number one cause of death in this game is showing up to a boss with the ammo in the wrong person's pockets.

## Inventory Management Without Item Boxes

Here is the change that trips up veterans hardest: Resident Evil 0 has no item boxes. Every item you drop lands on the floor and stays in that room for the rest of the game, unless a scripted sequence moves you somewhere you cannot return from. Each character has six slots, and several key items occupy two of them.

That means your inventory plan *is* your route plan. Community reports often describe the mid-game as a "moving day" where you shuttle gear between rooms you know you will revisit.

| Item Type | Keep on Hand? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Handgun plus its ammo | Yes, always | Your baseline damage and most common ammo |
| Shotgun or heavy weapon | Yes, on Billy | Emergency crowd control |
| Mixed healing items | Yes, at least two | One bad room can drain a full health bar |
| Hookshot | Only when needed | Takes two slots; drop it the moment the gap is crossed |
| Unused ammo types | No | Store them in a hub room you will pass through again |
| Spare key items | No | Once a door is open, the key is dead weight |

### Five Inventory Habits That Save Runs

1. **Pick a hub room per area.** The main hall of the training facility and the train's central cars make excellent stashes.
2. **Dump the Hookshot immediately after use.** It is one of the most complained-about items in the game for a reason.
3. **Do not carry duplicate weapons.** Two handguns do not kill faster; they just eat four slots.
4. **Drop items before cutscene-heavy rooms.** Some sequences relocate you, and anything left behind is gone.
5. **Save before a big item shuffle.** Ink ribbons are limited, but so is your patience for redoing a twenty-minute route.

## Weapons, Ammo, and Combat Economy

Resident Evil 0 gives you a decent arsenal, but the ammo economy is tight. You will almost never have enough of the good stuff, so the goal is to use cheap rounds on cheap enemies and save the heavy ordnance for the things that deserve it.

| Weapon | Typical Owner | Best Use |
|---|---|---|
| Knife | Both | Free damage; finish off downed or crawling enemies |
| Handgun | Rebecca (early), either later | Standard zombies, ammo is plentiful |
| Shotgun | Billy | Close-range burst, leech crowds, tight corridors |
| Grenade Launcher | Either | Groups of enemies and mid-tier monsters |
| Magnum | Either | Bosses only; ammo is rare |
| Submachine gun | Either | Panic moments, but it burns ammo fast |
| Rocket Launcher | Final area | Boss finisher; treat every shot as precious |

**Combat tips that hold up across difficulties:**

- Aim for stagger, not kills. A staggered zombie is a zombie you can walk past.
- Do not waste magnum or grenade rounds on regular enemies. You will regret it two rooms before the final boss.
- Some leech-type enemies have a habit of getting back up. Players report that fire-based finishing methods are more reliable than bullets for putting them down for good.
- Retreat through doors. Enemy AI resets, and you get free repositioning.

## Boss Fights: What to Expect and How to Prep

This section stays spoiler-light, but you should know the shape of each major encounter so you are not learning it with an empty shotgun.

| Boss | Location | Prep and Strategy |
|---|---|---|
| Leech humanoid | End of the train section | Keep your distance, circle constantly, and lean on handgun and shotgun rounds |
| Infected bat | Training facility | Dodge the swoops, then unload heavy ammo during its landing windows |
| Giant spider | Training facility | Keep moving to avoid webbing, use the shotgun at close range |
| Prototype Tyrant | Underground laboratory | Save magnum and grenade rounds; punish it after its charge attack |
| Final boss | Endgame area | Two-phase fight — bring maximum healing and your best remaining ammo |

Tough regular enemies deserve a mention too. The Eliminators (fast, aggressive primates) and the Plague Crawlers punish standing still, while Hunters can decapitate an underprepared character in a single lunge. When you see one of these, decide immediately whether you are fighting or running. Indecision is what kills you.

## Puzzles, Saving, and Speed Tips

Resident Evil 0's puzzles are mostly about item routing rather than brain-teasers. The chemical mixing sequence in the laboratory is the most involved: Rebecca combines colored chemicals to produce the compound needed to progress, and getting the order wrong wastes a trip back to the storage room.

| Situation | Smart Move |
|---|---|
| Before a boss door | Save, then move all heavy ammo onto the character you will control |
| Low on ink ribbons | Save only before bosses and before irreversible route decisions |
| Long backtrack ahead | Consolidate dropped items into one hub room first |
| Puzzle requires chemistry | Send Rebecca; Billy cannot complete it |
| Down to your last healing item | Mix herbs with Rebecca rather than using singles |

If you are chasing a faster clear time, the biggest wins come from route planning, not combat skill. Sketch your item drops, avoid unnecessary trips, and resist the urge to kill everything you see.

## Unlockables and Replay Value

Beating Resident Evil 0 is not the end. The game rewards completion with extra content that changes how a second run feels.

| Unlockable | How to Get It | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| Leech Hunter minigame | Finish the main story | A separate challenge mode where you collect leech charms across the training facility |
| Bonus weapons and costumes | Clear the game and complete Leech Hunter objectives | Makes a hard-difficulty run far more manageable |
| Wesker Mode (HD Remaster) | Complete the campaign | Play as Albert Wesker alongside Rebecca, with a very different toolkit |

Leech Hunter is the highlight for most players. It strips the story away and turns the training facility into a compact, tense scavenger hunt. If you enjoy the main game's item management, this mode is the purest version of it.

## FAQ

**Do I need to play Resident Evil 1 before Resident Evil 0?**
No, but it helps. Resident Evil 0 is a prequel, so the story stands alone — however, the fixed-camera controls, ink ribbon saving, and puzzle logic are all inherited from the original game. If you are using this Resident Evil 0 guide as your entry point, expect a steeper learning curve than the modern remakes.

**Why does Resident Evil 0 not have item boxes?**
It was a deliberate design choice to push the two-character mechanic further. Instead of teleporting gear around the map, you physically move it, which makes the Partner Zapping System matter for logistics as well as combat.

**Who should carry the weapons — Rebecca or Billy?**
Billy. He has more health, so he can survive the mistakes that come with front-line fighting. Give Rebecca the handgun, the healing stock, and any chemistry-related key items.

**Is Leech Hunter worth unlocking?**
For most players, yes. It is short, focused, and rewards the exact skills this Resident Evil 0 guide emphasizes: route planning, ammo discipline, and knowing when to run instead of fight.
