Resident Evil 0 is famous for its partner-swapping survival horror, but the game hides a second layer of content that plenty of players never touch. Understanding the Resident Evil 0 extra game lineup — the bonus modes and unlockables that open up after the credits roll — is the difference between finishing one campaign and squeezing far more out of a survival horror classic. The Resident Evil 0 extra game package is small on paper: one score-chasing mini-game, one remaster-exclusive power fantasy, and a set of rewards that permanently change how the main story plays.

Why does any of this matter? Because the base campaign is genuinely demanding. Ammo is scarce, the item box is gone, and you're juggling two inventories at once while swapping between Rebecca Chambers and Billy Coop. Bonus content gives you a reason to return with new rules and new tools — and the rewards you earn make a second, harder playthrough far more bearable than the first.

## What Counts as Resident Evil 0 Extra Game Content?

Not everything in the bonus package is a full mode. Some of it is unlockable gear, some is cosmetic, and some is an entirely separate scenario with its own rules. Here's the lay of the land.

| Extra content | Where it appears | How it unlocks | What you actually do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leech Hunter | Every version, from GameCube onward | Finish the main story once | Hunt down 100 leeches in a hostile Training Facility |
| Wesker Mode | HD Remaster and later ports | Finish the main story once | Replay the campaign with Albert Wesker as Rebecca's partner |
| Bonus weapons | Main campaign | Strong Leech Hunter results | Carry powerful firepower into a repeat run |
| Alternate costumes | Most versions | Completion milestones | Swap Rebecca's and Billy's outfits |
| Harder replays | Main campaign | Available from the start or after a clear, depending on version | Test your routing with thinner resources |

It's worth clarifying the terminology, because "extra game" gets used loosely. Players generally mean anything outside the main scenario, while different releases label the same content differently in menus. Whether you call it bonus content, a mini-game, or a side mode, it all lives in the same corner of the package — and it all unlocks the same way: by finishing the story.

Version matters here. The original GameCube release shipped with Leech Hunter and the usual unlockables. The 2016 HD Remaster brought updated visuals, widescreen support, and one genuinely new addition in Wesker Mode. If you're playing a modern port, you're getting the fullest version of the Resident Evil 0 extra game content available.

## Leech Hunter: The Original Resident Evil 0 Extra Game

Leech Hunter is the mode most people mean when they search for the Resident Evil 0 extra game. It takes one of the game's biggest locations — the Umbrella Training Facility — and turns it into a scavenger hunt with teeth.

### How the mode works

- You choose either Billy or Rebecca before the run begins, then play solo with no partner to cover you.
- The map is a rearranged version of the Training Facility, repopulated with enemies.
- Your objective is to find and collect 100 leeches scattered across the rooms.
- You begin with minimal supplies, so every fight becomes a spending decision.
- Health and ammunition are limited, and the mode punishes careless backtracking.
- Your final results feed directly into rewards for the main campaign.

The theme ties neatly back to the story. Leech imagery runs through the entire game thanks to Dr. Marcus and his research, so a mode built around collecting the creatures feels like a natural extension of the world rather than a bolted-on extra.

Community reports consistently describe Leech Hunter as the most demanding content in the package. The tension comes from the route itself: rooms you cleared on the way in are dangerous again on the way out, and returning through them with a full inventory is where most runs fall apart. Player experience also suggests the collected leeches themselves eat into your carrying space, which turns every pickup into a trade-off between progress and preparedness.

### Leech Hunter at a glance

| Detail | What to expect |
|---|---|
| Playable character | Billy or Rebecca, chosen at the start |
| Objective | Collect all 100 leeches |
| Setting | A hostile version of the Training Facility |
| Combat pressure | Constant — enemies are dense and supplies are thin |
| Inventory | Tight by design; you can't carry everything you find |
| Reward type | Main-game weapons tied to your results |
| Difficulty vs. campaign | Widely considered harder than the story |

### Practical tips for a clean run

- **Learn the map first.** Route knowledge beats reflexes. If you can picture the facility's loops, you'll waste far fewer supplies.
- **Decide what to skip.** Not every enemy needs to die. Dodging is a skill in this mode, not a shortcut.
- **Think about your carrying space.** Every leech you pick up is inventory you can't use for healing or ammo.
- **Save your best weapons.** Dense rooms and tougher enemies deserve the heavy firepower.
- **Plan the split.** If you're chasing a specific reward, decide early which character collects the bulk of the leeches.
- **Run it twice.** One attempt to learn the layout, one attempt to perform.

## Wesker Mode: The Remaster's Headline Extra Game

If you're playing the HD Remaster, Wesker Mode is the marquee addition — and it's the one piece of bonus content that didn't exist in the original 2002 release. The premise is simple: Albert Wesker replaces Billy Coop as Rebecca's partner, and the campaign plays out with the same story beats but a very different power curve.

| Feature | Standard campaign | Wesker Mode |
|---|---|---|
| Partner | Billy Coop | Albert Wesker |
| Signature ability | None beyond standard firearms and the knife | A charge attack that instantly destroys most standard enemies |
| Visual signature | — | Glowing red eyes |
| Story | Original scenario | Same scenario, new partner |
| Availability | All versions | HD Remaster and later ports |
| Best for | First-time players | Veterans who want a power trip |

The charge attack is the headline. It lets you delete ordinary enemies instead of spending bullets on them, which flips the game's resource math on its head. Rooms that felt like attrition battles in the normal campaign become quick clears, and suddenly you're the most dangerous thing in the Arklay Mountains.

It's not a harder mode — it's a victory lap, and a genuinely good way to see the campaign again with fresh eyes. Because Wesker handles the combat so differently, you'll find yourself taking routes and risks you'd never attempt as Billy.

Worth knowing: Wesker Mode is a remaster feature. If you're playing the original GameCube disc, you won't find it, which is a common source of confusion for players hunting down the Resident Evil 0 extra game content they remember hearing about.

## Rewards and Unlockables: What You Actually Earn

The Resident Evil 0 extra game isn't just for fun — it feeds back into the main campaign. Leech Hunter results translate into bonus weaponry for a repeat playthrough, and the reward you receive depends on how many leeches you collect and how the count is divided between the two characters. Player experience suggests it's worth planning which character carries the bulk of the collection if you're chasing the strongest payout.

| Unlockable | How to get it | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Leech Hunter mode | Clear the main story | Opens the package's biggest challenge |
| Wesker Mode | Clear the main story (HD Remaster) | A whole second way to play the campaign |
| Bonus main-game weapon | Strong Leech Hunter performance | Turns a punishing replay into a manageable one |
| Alternate costumes | Completion milestones | Cosmetic variety for repeat runs |
| Replay incentive | Built into the structure | Gives veterans a reason to return |

A word of caution: detailed reward charts have been documented by the community for years, and they're worth checking before you commit to a route. The gap between a good run and a perfect one can be an entire weapon you'll use for the rest of the campaign — which makes Leech Hunter one of the highest-value side modes in the series.

Cosmetics shouldn't be dismissed either. Swapping outfits for Rebecca and Billy gives a repeat playthrough a different feel, and since these unlocks are typically tied to completion milestones, you'll pick most of them up naturally on the way to the bigger rewards.

## Tips for Getting the Most Out of Every Resident Evil 0 Extra Game Mode

- **Finish the campaign on Normal first.** Both major modes unlock from a single clear, and you'll want the map knowledge before you take on Leech Hunter.
- **Treat Leech Hunter as a routing puzzle, not a shooter.** Ammo is a resource, and killing everything is rarely the answer.
- **Use Wesker Mode as a victory lap.** It's the perfect way to revisit the story after a tough first clear, or to farm confidence before a hard run.
- **Watch your partner.** Even in bonus content, the partner-swap system shapes what you can carry and where you can go.
- **Don't ignore the rewards.** A bonus weapon changes the pacing of a hard replay dramatically — and makes the toughest sections survivable.
- **Check your version.** Not every release includes every mode, and knowing what you own saves a lot of frustration.

| Common mistake | Better approach |
|---|---|
| Fighting every enemy in Leech Hunter | Dodge and conserve ammunition |
| Rushing into the Training Facility blind | Learn the layout on a normal run first |
| Expecting Wesker Mode in the original release | Check which version you own |
| Ignoring the leech split between characters | Plan who collects what before you start |
| Skipping the rewards entirely | Farm Leech Hunter early for a smoother replay |

## FAQ

**Is there an extra game in Resident Evil 0?**
Yes. The package includes Leech Hunter, a collectathon mini-game set in the Training Facility, plus Wesker Mode in the HD Remaster. Both unlock after you finish the main story once.

**How do you unlock Leech Hunter?**
Complete the main campaign one time. The mode then becomes available from the menu, and you can choose Billy or Rebecca before the run begins. There's no shortcut — a single clear is the only requirement.

**Is Wesker Mode in the original GameCube version?**
No. Wesker Mode was added in the 2016 HD Remaster and appears in later ports. The original release includes Leech Hunter and the standard unlockables, but not the alternate partner campaign.

**Do rewards from the Resident Evil 0 extra game carry into the main campaign?**
Yes — strong Leech Hunter performance unlocks bonus weapons for a repeat playthrough of the main story, which is the main reason the mode is worth mastering. Plan your route, protect your ammo, and the payoff follows you straight into the campaign.
