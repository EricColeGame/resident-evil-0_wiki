Resident Evil 0 has a reputation as one of the hardest games in the classic survival horror lineup, and its difficulty settings are a big part of that. Picking among the Resident Evil 0 difficulty levels changes enemy toughness, how scarce ammo and healing items become, and how badly a single bad decision hurts you. This guide breaks down how the Resident Evil 0 difficulty levels work in both the original GameCube release and the 2016 HD remaster, what each one asks of you, and which one you should pick first.

## How Resident Evil 0 Difficulty Levels Change the Game

Resident Evil 0 is a prequel set in July 1998, following S.T.A.R.S. medic Rebecca Chambers and escaped convict Billy Coen from a stalled train to the Umbrella Management Training Facility. Its signature twist is that item boxes do not exist. Anything you drop stays on the floor of the room where you left it, and each character only has six inventory slots. Every pickup is a trade-off, which is exactly why the difficulty setting matters so much here.

The mode you select touches four things: enemy durability and damage output, the drop rate for ammo, gunpowder, herbs, and ink ribbons, how aggressively enemies pursue you, and how forgiving the save economy feels. Higher settings do not add new puzzles or change the story — they tighten the margins until every fight is a budget decision.

| System | Lower difficulty | Higher difficulty |
|---|---|---|
| Enemy health | Drops fast to handgun rounds | Soaks bullets; every kill is expensive |
| Enemy damage | Forgiving hits | Two or three hits can put you in real danger |
| Ammo and gunpowder | Plentiful drops | Scarce; crafting becomes mandatory |
| Healing items | Green herbs show up often | You ration, mix, and hoard |
| Enemy aggression | Fewer ambushes | More pursuit and tighter chokepoints |
| Save resources | Comfortable | Ink ribbons feel like actual currency |

Capcom has never published exact damage or drop-rate tables for the game, so the comparisons below reflect player experience and community reports gathered across the original release and the remaster. If you want the official product details, the [official Resident Evil series site](https://www.residentevil.com/) is the place to start.

## Resident Evil 0 Difficulty Levels Compared

Here is the full picture. Note that Wesker Mode and Leech Hunter are bonus content rather than standard difficulty tiers — they sit on top of whatever base mode you choose.

| Difficulty | Availability | Overall feel | Resource flow | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|

### Easy Mode: Story First, Survival Second

Easy exists to let you experience Rebecca and Billy's story without fighting the inventory system for every inch of progress. Enemies fall faster, you absorb more punishment, and supplies show up more often. It was added with the HD remaster specifically to lower the barrier for newcomers.

Do not mistake it for a walk in the park. The lack of item boxes still applies, so a careless drop can still cost you a five-minute backtrack. Leech Zombies, Eliminators, and the late-game Tyrant encounters can still punish sloppy positioning. Easy lowers the ceiling on frustration; it does not remove the survival horror bones of the game.

### Normal Mode: The Balanced Baseline

Normal is the version most players should start with, and it is the closest thing to the developers' intended experience. Ammo is tight enough that you will think about it, but generous enough that a careful player rarely gets stuck. You will probably craft gunpowder into handgun ammo more than once, dodge a few fights you could technically win, and lean on the character-switching system.

The tension curve here is the best in the game. Early encounters feel threatening, mid-game bosses demand preparation, and the final stretch rewards anyone who has been managing herbs and inventory space all along.

### Hard Mode: Where the Survival Horror Reputation Comes From

Hard is the mode that gave Resident Evil 0 its reputation. Enemies hit harder, resources dry up, and the cost of a wasted shotgun shell is measured in backtracking. Fights that are optional on Normal become fights you should actively avoid.

Two habits carry Hard runs. First, route around encounters whenever a door lets you — many enemies can simply be left behind. Second, plan saves. Wasting an ink ribbon on a safe room you will revisit in three minutes is the kind of small error that compounds over a long campaign.

## Unlockable Modes: Wesker Mode and Leech Hunter

Beyond the standard tiers, Resident Evil 0 hides two substantial bonus modes behind a single completion of the main story. They are not alternative difficulty levels in the traditional sense, but they dramatically change how a second run feels.

| Mode | How to unlock | What it is | Why play it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wesker Mode | Finish the main story once | A bonus scenario starring Albert Wesker with Rebecca as his partner; Wesker has a dash and a charged power-up | A power fantasy and a genuinely different second run |
| Leech Hunter | Finish the main story once | A minigame where you hunt down green and blue leech charms using both Billy and Rebecca | Sharpens routing and map knowledge, plus bonus rewards |

Wesker Mode replaces Billy with a superhuman version of Wesker who can close distance instantly and hit far harder than the normal cast. It is not balanced for tension — it is balanced for spectacle, and it is a great way to see how much you actually learned about the level layouts.

Leech Hunter strips out the story entirely and turns the training facility into a timed scavenger hunt. Community reports consistently describe it as the best routing practice in the game, and clearing it with a full collection unlocks extra rewards for the main campaign.

Costumes and other extras also unlock as you complete runs, so there is a steady drip of reasons to return after your first credits roll.

## Survival Tips That Work on Every Difficulty

These habits pay off regardless of which of the Resident Evil 0 difficulty levels you select.

- **Split your ammo.** Do not let one character carry everything. If Billy goes down in a bad room, you want Rebecca to have something to work with.
- **Treat key rooms as item boxes.** Drop spare ammo and herbs in a room you know you will revisit, then travel light through new territory.
- **Use Rebecca for herb mixing.** She can combine herbs on the spot, which frees up slots and turns three green herbs into a much better heal.
- **Use Billy for muscle and front-line combat.** He moves heavy objects and absorbs hits better, so let him take point in dangerous corridors.
- **Craft deliberately.** Gunpowder is not a collectible — it is a decision. Convert it into the ammo type your current situation actually needs.
- **Walk away from fights.** Many encounters can be avoided entirely by simply leaving the room. On Hard, that is often the correct play.
- **Save with intent.** Plan your typewriter stops around bosses, long treks, and new areas rather than saving on reflex.

| Normal | Fight only when necessary | Mix herbs into stronger versions | Save before bosses and long journeys |
| Hard | Avoid most fights; craft carefully | Ration heavily; never waste a mixed herb | Save at planned milestones only |

## Which Difficulty Should You Choose First?

The honest answer depends on your history with the series, not your pride.

| Your situation | Recommended pick |
|---|---|
| First time playing any classic Resident Evil | Easy |
| Played the original trilogy or RE1 HD Remaster | Normal |
| Finished Resident Evil 0 before and want the tension back | Hard |
| Want a victory lap with a superhuman protagonist | Wesker Mode |
| Want to master the map and routing | Leech Hunter |

If you only plan to play once, Normal is the sweet spot. Easy is a legitimate choice for anyone who wants the story of Rebecca and Billy without the resource anxiety, and there is no penalty for taking it. Hard is best saved for a second run, when you already know where the dangerous rooms are and which fights you can simply walk past.

## FAQ

**How many difficulty levels does Resident Evil 0 have?**

The 2016 HD remaster offers Easy, Normal, and Hard from the difficulty menu, plus the unlockable Wesker Mode and the Leech Hunter minigame. The original GameCube release shipped with a smaller set of options, and Easy was added later to make the game more welcoming to newcomers.

**Can you change the Resident Evil 0 difficulty levels in the middle of a playthrough?**

No. Difficulty is locked in the moment you start a new game, so if you want to switch from Hard to Normal, you will need to begin a fresh save file. That is worth remembering before you commit to a punishing run on a whim.

**Is Hard mode unlocked from the start?**

In the HD remaster, Hard can be selected when you begin a new game, so you can jump straight into it. That said, it is not recommended for a first playthrough — the resource scarcity assumes you already understand gunpowder crafting, herb mixing, and the partner system.

**Does Easy mode lock you out of achievements or bonus content?**

No. Easy still lets you complete the story, collect files, and unlock Wesker Mode and Leech Hunter. A few of the tougher completion goals expect a Hard-mode clear, so players chasing a full achievement list will eventually need to take on the harder setting — but nothing story-related is gated behind it.
