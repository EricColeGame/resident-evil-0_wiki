Most players finish Resident Evil 0, watch the credits roll, and move on. The ones who linger discover that the real endurance test is tucked away in the bonus menu. **Resident Evil 0 Leech Hunter** strips out the story, drops Billy Coen and Rebecca Chambers into the Umbrella Executive Training Center, and asks them to sweep the entire facility for 100 leech charms while the game quietly turns up the pressure around them.

It matters because this mode is not filler. **Resident Evil 0 Leech Hunter** is the gate to the campaign's best bonus rewards, and it doubles as a masterclass in the resource management that defines the whole series. If you want serious firepower in the main story — or just want to find out how well you really know the training facility — this is the challenge you have to beat.

## What Is Leech Hunter in Resident Evil 0?

Leech Hunter is an unlockable bonus mode that becomes available after you finish the main campaign once. There's no story framing, no cutscenes, and no partner AI doing the work for you. You get a stripped-down version of the training facility, a pair of characters, and one objective: collect every leech charm on the map and get both survivors to the exit.

The twist is that you control both Billy and Rebecca, switching between them at the press of a button. The character you aren't controlling stays exactly where you left them — standing still, completely exposed. That single design decision is what makes the mode so tense. Every room you clear is a room where one of your two characters is parked, waiting to be mauled.

Because the map is recycled from the campaign, veterans have a real advantage. If you already know how the main hall, the save rooms, and the long corridors connect, you know half the route before you start.

## How Leech Charms and Character Swapping Work

In Resident Evil 0 Leech Hunter, there are 100 leech charms hidden across the facility, split evenly into two colors, and each color is locked to one character. Rebecca can only pick up green charms; Billy can only pick up blue ones. Walk Billy over a green charm and nothing happens — the pickup prompt simply never appears.

That rule forces you to move both characters through every wing of the building, which is exactly what the mode wants. You can't speed through it with one survivor while the other hides in a closet.

| Charm type | Who can collect it | Total | Key implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| Green leech charms | Rebecca Chambers | 50 | Rebecca must physically reach every green pickup |
| Blue leech charms | Billy Coen | 50 | Billy must do the same for the blue ones |
| Combined | Both characters | 100 | Every room matters; skipping a wing caps your rank |

Swapping is instant, and both characters share the same screen. The inactive one doesn't follow you — they stand frozen until you take control again. Get into the habit of parking the idle character in a corner of a cleared room rather than in a hallway, and never leave them somewhere enemies are likely to respawn.

Items can be dropped and picked up by the other character, so ammo and healing can be shuffled between Billy and Rebecca as needed. Treat that as your logistics system: if Rebecca is about to sweep a monster-heavy area, hand her the shotgun first.

## Enemies, Difficulty Scaling, and the Leech Man

The difficulty curve in Resident Evil 0 Leech Hunter is tied directly to your progress. The more leeches you bank, the more hostile the facility becomes. Rooms that were quiet on your first pass can be crawling with enemies when you double back, so a route that works early can become a trap later.

| Threat | Behavior | How to handle it |
|---|---|---|
| Zombies | Slow, numerous, punishing in corridors | Headshots when you can afford them, walk past when you can't |
| Fast creatures | Close distance quickly and hit hard | Shotgun or heavy weapon; don't try to outrun them in tight spaces |
| The Leech Man | A relentless pursuer that stalks the facility | Don't fight it — leave the room, and never corner yourself |
| Crowded rooms | Multiple enemies stacked in one space | Clear from a doorway, retreat, then repeat |

The Leech Man is the star of the show and the biggest rookie trap. New players dump entire magazines into it, run dry, and then die to a zombie three rooms later. It's built to be avoided, not killed. If it shows up, relocate.

Ammo management is the real difficulty setting here. Because you're splitting resources between two characters while the enemy count keeps climbing, every missed shotgun blast is a tax you'll pay later.

## Ranks and Rewards: What a Perfect Run Unlocks

Ranks and rewards are the reason most players stick with Resident Evil 0 Leech Hunter long after the credits. Finishing the mode isn't the same as finishing it well. Your rank depends on how many leech charms you collected and whether both characters made it out alive. The top tier — all 100 charms banked and a clean escape — is the one everyone chases, because it unlocks the campaign's headline bonus.

| Tier | How it's earned | What players report |
|---|---|---|
| Incomplete | A low charm count, or one character dies | No meaningful bonus; treat it as a practice run |
| Partial | Most charms collected but a few missed | Some unlocks, with results that vary between versions |
| Perfect | All 100 charms plus a successful escape | The big prize: infinite ammunition for the main campaign |

Community reports on exact thresholds differ slightly between the original release and later ports, so check the bonus menu on your own version before committing to a specific target. The safe rule is simple: get all 100 charms, and keep both characters breathing.

It's worth stressing that these bonuses carry into the main story, not just the bonus mode. That's why the mode has stayed relevant for decades — it isn't a throwaway extra, it's a reward loop that changes how the campaign plays.

## Tips, Routes, and Loadouts for a Clean 100-Leech Run

A perfect Resident Evil 0 Leech Hunter run is a planning problem, not a shooting problem. The players who clear it consistently do three things: they sweep the map in phases, they keep the idle character safe, and they hoard ammo like it's currency.

### Phase-by-phase plan

| Phase | Focus | Common mistake |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | Grab weapons, ammo, and healing; learn the layout | Burning ammo on enemies you can simply avoid |
| Mid-run | Sweep rooms wing by wing, banking every charm you find | Leaving the inactive character in an open corridor |
| Late run | Mop up skipped rooms and prepare for heavier spawns | Getting greedy in a room you don't actually need |
| Escape | Bring both characters to the exit together | Forgetting the second character and losing the run |

### Loadout priorities

| Priority | Item | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Shotgun or heavy weapon | Answers fast enemies and crowded rooms |
| 2 | Handgun ammo | Your cheap, reliable fallback |
| 3 | Green herbs | Combine before you need them, not during a fight |
| 4 | First aid spray | Emergency only; don't burn it on a scratch |

A few habits separate clean runs from frustrating ones. Check your map constantly — the mode rewards route efficiency more than reflexes. Drop items for your partner before a big sweep instead of carrying everything on one character. And when the Leech Man appears, treat it as a signal to relocate rather than a boss encounter.

## FAQ

**How do you unlock Resident Evil 0 Leech Hunter?**
Complete the main campaign once. The mode then appears in the bonus menu, and you can replay it as often as you like without touching your story save.

**Is Resident Evil 0 Leech Hunter co-op?**
No. It's strictly single-player. You control both Billy and Rebecca by swapping between them, and the character you aren't controlling stands still until you switch back.

**What happens if you collect all 100 leeches in Resident Evil 0 Leech Hunter?**
You earn the mode's top rank, which the community widely reports unlocks infinite ammunition for the main campaign. Both characters must escape alive for the run to count.

**Is Leech Hunter included in the HD remaster?**
Yes. The modern release includes the bonus mode alongside the main story, with updated visuals, a modern control option, and achievements.
