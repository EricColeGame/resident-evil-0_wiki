The Ecliptic Express is where Resident Evil 0 stops being a mood piece and starts testing you. This Resident Evil 0 walkthrough train guide covers the game's entire opening chapter — the infected passenger cars, the leech-infested corridors, the access puzzles that gate your way toward the front of the train, and the crash that dumps you into Umbrella's backyard. Get this section wrong and you'll limp into the next several hours with no ammo, no herbs, and a partner stranded in a hallway you can't reach. Get it right, and you'll understand why the Resident Evil 0 walkthrough train sequence is still one of the most demanding openings in classic survival horror.

The train chapter is short on space and long on consequences. There are no shops, no item boxes, and very few places to run. Everything you decide in the first hour echoes through the rest of the game.

## Why the Ecliptic Express Is the Hardest Opening in Classic Resident Evil

Resident Evil 0 originally launched on the GameCube in 2002 and came back in a 2016 HD remaster for modern platforms. Either way, the story opens in July 1998, days before the mansion incident, with Rebecca Chambers — an 18-year-old S.T.A.R.S. Bravo Team medic — boarding a stalled Umbrella luxury train in the Arklay Mountains. She is alone, lightly armed, and the passengers are already dead or turning.

What makes the train so punishing is geometry. The Ecliptic Express is a chain of narrow corridors where you can't easily circle around a grab, and doors funnel you into enemies instead of away from them. Combine that with a partner system that splits your resources across two inventories, and you get a chapter that punishes hoarding and panic in equal measure.

| Chapter detail | What it means for your run |
| --- | --- |
| Setting | Umbrella's Ecliptic Express, Arklay Mountains, July 1998 |
| Playable characters | Rebecca Chambers and Billy Coen, switchable at any time |
| Storage | No item boxes — dropped items stay in the room where you left them |

| Resource density | Low ammo, moderate herbs, very few healing items early |
| Biggest failure point | Spending shotgun and heavy ammo on ordinary passenger zombies |

## Partner Zapping and Inventory Rules You Must Master First

Resident Evil 0's signature mechanic is Partner Zapping: you control both characters and can swap between them instantly, with the AI handling whoever you leave behind. The train is the tutorial for this, whether it admits it or not. One character can hold a position while the other loots a car, and a well-placed swap can save you from a grab you never saw coming.

The AI partner is not a combat genius. Left alone in a room with a zombie, your partner will take hits. The practical rule: never park a character somewhere enemies can reach, and never leave a character standing between you and your only exit.

### The Drop-Item System Replaces Item Boxes

Because Resident Evil 0 has no item boxes, your inventory is effectively the floor. Anything you drop stays exactly where it lands, which means you can build a supply cache in a room you pass through repeatedly — the dining car or a central passenger car is ideal. Just remember that the train is destroyed at the end of the chapter, so anything you leave behind when the crash happens is gone for good.

| Character | Strengths | Best use on the train |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rebecca Chambers | Herb mixing, combining items, support role | Carry all herbs, mixed healing items, and puzzle items |
| Billy Coen | More health, better damage soak, frontline fighter | Carry the main weapon, spare ammo, and take point in corridors |

## Resident Evil 0 Walkthrough Train Route: Phase-by-Phase Objectives

Exact puzzle steps vary depending on how thoroughly you explore, but the chapter always follows the same broad beats. Treat the table below as your route map rather than a strict script.

| Phase | Main objective | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Boarding | Explore the rear passenger cars and learn the drop system | Passenger zombies in tight aisles; don't waste ammo on downed bodies |
| 2. First contact | Meet Billy Coen and agree to work together | Scripted scene — keep your handgun loaded before triggering it |
| 3. Cooperation | Free Billy and split gear between two inventories | You lose solo control; assign weapons deliberately |
| 4. Supply sweep | Clear the passenger and dining cars for keys, ammo, and herbs | Full inventories — drop, don't discard |
| 5. Access puzzles | Solve the local mechanisms that open the way toward the front | Leech ambushes and long backtracking routes |
| 6. Rear car | Survive the leech creature that takes over the back of the train | Heavy resource drain; keep both characters healed |
| 7. Brakes and crash | Reach the controls, ride out the derailment, and survive the transition | Final scripted sequence; uncollected items are lost |

A few habits make all seven phases easier:

- Sweep a car completely before triggering anything that looks like a cutscene. Scripted moments often lock doors behind you.
- Keep Rebecca's inventory light so she has room to pick up puzzle items on the spot.
- Leave a breadcrumb trail: drop spare ammo and healing items in the car you pass through most, not in a dead-end cabin.
- Reload before opening doors. RE0 zombies love a doorway grab.

## Train Enemies, Herb Mixing, and Ammo Discipline

The train's enemy roster is small but efficient. You'll face infected passengers and crew, leech swarms that punish standing still, and a larger leech creature near the end of the chapter that many players treat as the game's first real skill check. Community reports consistently flag that fight as the point where under-prepared runs fall apart.

| Enemy | Where you meet it | Threat level | Best answer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Infected passengers | Passenger cars, dining car | Moderate | Handgun headshots; walk past downed enemies instead of finishing them |
| Leech swarms | Corridors and rear sections | High | Keep distance, avoid melee range, use your strongest available weapon |
| Large leech creature | Rear of the train | Boss-level | Full health on both characters, heavy weapon loaded, no wasted shots |
| Environment | Narrow aisles, doors, tight corners | Constant | Back up before firing; never let a partner stand in a doorway |

### Herb Mixing Basics

Rebecca is the herb mixer in this duo, so route every green, red, and blue herb into her inventory. Mixing before a fight is almost always better than healing mid-fight, because a mixed herb costs one turn and heals more than the sum of its parts.

| Combination | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Green | Small health recovery |
| Green + Green | Moderate health recovery |
| Green + Green + Green | Large health recovery |
| Green + Red | Full health recovery |
| Green + Blue | Health recovery plus poison cure |
| Green + Red + Blue | Full recovery plus poison cure |

Ammo discipline matters just as much. The handgun is your default answer on the train; anything heavier should be reserved for the leech encounters and the rear-car fight. If a hallway zombie can be walked around, walk around it.

## Difficulty, Ink Ribbons, and HD Remaster Differences

The 2016 remaster preserves the original's structure while adding modern conveniences, but the train chapter remains the difficulty filter it always was. Saving still costs ink ribbons on standard difficulties, so treat each typewriter as a resource rather than a checkpoint.

| Setting | What changes |
| --- | --- |
| Easy | Forgiving damage and more generous resources; good for a first story run |
| Normal | The intended experience; ink ribbons and ammo both feel tight |
| Hard | Ruthless enemy damage and scarce supplies; plan every encounter |
| Wesker Mode | Unlocked after beating the game; a completely different power fantasy |
| Leech Hunter | Bonus minigame unlocked after completion; pure score-attack challenge |

If you're playing the remaster, check the official Resident Evil series site from Capcom for platform listings and version details before you buy, since the game has shipped on several generations of hardware.

For a first playthrough, Normal is the right call. Easy can teach you bad habits — specifically, the habit of shooting everything — and the train is the worst place to learn that lesson.

## FAQ: Resident Evil 0 Walkthrough Train Questions

**How long does the train section take in a Resident Evil 0 walkthrough train run?**
Community reports put a careful first run at roughly an hour or more, depending on how much you explore and how often you backtrack for dropped items. Experienced players who know the route and the puzzle solutions move through it far faster.

**Can I skip the Ecliptic Express?**
No. It's the mandatory opening chapter of Resident Evil 0, and the items you collect there carry directly into the Umbrella facility that follows. The only train-related content you can unlock later is bonus material like Leech Hunter and Wesker Mode.

**Should Billy or Rebecca carry the herbs?**
Rebecca. She's the one who can mix herbs into stronger healing items, and she has the smaller effective health pool, so keeping recovery options on her is the safer play. Give Billy the weapons, ammo, and anything that needs muscle.

**Do items I drop on the train disappear?**
They stay in the room where you dropped them for as long as you're on the train. Once the chapter's crash sequence plays out, though, everything left behind is gone — so make a final sweep before you commit to the endgame of the chapter.

Master the train and the rest of Resident Evil 0 becomes a resource-management game you can actually win. Fumble it, and you'll spend the next several hours scavenging for handgun rounds in a facility full of things that don't miss. Slow down, split your gear with intent, and treat every bullet on the Ecliptic Express like it's the last one you'll ever find — because for a while, it might be.
