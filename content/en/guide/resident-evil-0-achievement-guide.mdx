Resident Evil 0 is the black sheep of the classic survival horror lineup — and arguably the hardest of the bunch to finish completely. Between a partner system that punishes careless play, a world with no item boxes, and a bonus mode that throws you into a mansion full of Hunters with almost nothing to defend yourself, earning every trophy takes planning. This Resident Evil 0 achievement guide is built to remove the guesswork: what to unlock first, which runs can be combined, where most players lose their streak, and how to survive Leech Hunter without throwing your controller. If you want one clean, efficient path to 100%, start here — the rest of this Resident Evil 0 achievement guide breaks the whole list into manageable chunks.

## Why Resident Evil 0 Is a Brutal Completion

Most Resident Evil games let you stash extra ammo in a magic box and forget about it. Resident Evil 0 removes that safety net entirely. Every item you pick up either stays in your inventory or gets dropped on the floor of the room you're standing in, and you'll need to remember exactly where you left that grenade launcher three hours ago. That single design choice makes routing, backtracking, and inventory management the core skill of every achievement run.

Add a second playable character who follows you, fights on their own, and can absolutely get themselves killed, and you have a game where sloppy habits get punished hard.

| Design element | What it means for achievements |
| --- | --- |
| No item boxes | You must plan drops; forgetting gear costs full backtracking trips |
| Partner swapping | Two inventories to manage, and both characters can die |
| Limited saves on higher difficulties | Ink ribbons force you to bank progress carefully |
| Slow, tank-style movement | Speed-related challenges require route memorization |
| Leech Hunter as a separate mode | A whole second skill set to master for the platinum |

## How the Achievement List Actually Breaks Down

The good news: the list is not as random as it looks. Almost every trophy falls into one of five buckets, and several of them overlap, meaning a single smart playthrough can clear multiple goals at once. Community reports consistently note that blind first runs waste hours — you'll redo content you could have finished the first time.

| Category | What it typically asks | Difficulty | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Story completion | Finish the campaign on each difficulty | Low to Moderate | Your first run teaches the map |
| Challenge runs | Restricted saves, healing limits, fast clears | High | Combine only after you know the routes |
| Leech Hunter | Collect all leeches and clear the bonus mode | High | Requires its own practice sessions |
| Unlockables | Wesker Mode, bonus weapons, costumes | Low | Mostly free rewards from story clears |
| Collection & exploration | Files, items, optional pickups | Low | Easy to sweep during a relaxed run |

A practical rule of thumb: treat your first run as a scouting trip, not an achievement run. Learn enemy placements, learn where the key items spawn, and learn which rooms you'd rather never revisit.

## The Efficient Playthrough Roadmap

Order matters more here than in almost any other Resident Evil game, because unlocks feed into each other. Beating the campaign opens the bonus content; bonus content makes later challenge runs dramatically easier. Trying to force a hard-mode run before you have those tools is the single most common reason people burn out.

| Step | Difficulty | Main focus | What you gain |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Easy | Explore fully, read files, learn routes | Map knowledge and unlock access |
| 2 | Easy or Normal | Clean up story trophies, grab collectibles | Any missed files and optional items |
| 3 | Leech Hunter | Learn spawn behavior, clear with a full leech haul | Best bonus weapon rewards |
| 4 | Hard | Story completion plus any difficulty trophies | Final story-based achievements |
| 5 | Hard or Normal | Challenge restrictions and speed | Last remaining trophies |

### Difficulty Tips That Save Hours

- On Easy, use the breathing room to practice the train section, since the opening chapter is a tutorial for the entire game's pacing.
- Rebecca can mix herbs into stronger healing items; Billy cannot. Keep your best healing on Rebecca and your heavy weapons on Billy.
- Billy takes more punishment and is the better front-line fighter. Let him tank, and keep Rebecca out of grab range.
- Drop spare weapons in rooms you know you'll pass through again, not in dead-end areas.
- If you're chasing a low-save run, plan your save points around boss fights, not around distance traveled.

## Leech Hunter: The Biggest Trophy Block

Leech Hunter is where most completion attempts stall. It's a self-contained mode set in the training facility mansion, where you control both Rebecca and Billy and must collect all 100 leeches scattered across the map while enemies hunt you. Reach the exit with the full haul, and you're rewarded with the mode's best unlockables — including infinite-ammo bonuses that make later story runs much easier. Community reports suggest many players need several attempts before their first successful full collection, so don't treat a failed run as wasted time.

| Leech Hunter rule | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Playable characters | Rebecca and Billy, switchable at will |
| Objective | Collect all 100 leeches, then escape |
| Inventories | Separate for each character — split your gear wisely |
| Main threats | Hunters, zombies, and other returning enemies from the main game |
| Failure condition | Either character dying ends the attempt |
| Rewards | Bonus weapons tied to how many leeches you collected |

### Leech Hunter Survival Strategy

- Clear rooms before you loot them. Rushing a leech pickup with a Hunter still alive is how runs end.
- Split ammo types between Rebecca and Billy so one death doesn't wipe out your entire arsenal.
- Keep healing items on whichever character you're actively controlling during a fight.
- Learn which rooms are dead ends and loot those early, while your resources are healthiest.
- If you're close to a full collection and low on supplies, consider finishing the run and banking the partial reward rather than gambling on the last few leeches.

## Wesker Mode, Bonus Weapons, and Cleanup

Once the campaign is done, the game hands you several tools that make remaining trophies far more comfortable. Wesker Mode is the headline unlock: you replay the story with Albert Wesker in Billy's place, complete with a dash move and a special attack that changes how you approach encounters. It's a great way to sweep missed collectibles because the added mobility cuts travel time significantly.

| Unlock | How it's earned | Best use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wesker Mode | Finish the main story | Faster cleanup runs and collectible sweeps |
| Leech Hunter access | Finish the main story | Bonus weapon rewards |
| Bonus weapons | Leech Hunter performance | Trivializes Hard-mode combat |
| Costumes and extras | Story and mode completion | Cosmetic completion |

If you're playing the HD remaster on modern platforms, keep an eye on your platform's achievement list for the exact wording of each challenge — some restrictions are stricter than the in-game description implies. ## Common Mistakes That Cost You Trophies

Every failed run traces back to a handful of habits. Fix these and your completion rate jumps immediately.

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hoarding ammo | You finish the game with 200 handgun rounds and no trophies | Use ammo; spare supplies earn nothing |
| Dropping items in dead ends | Full backtracking trips eat hours | Drop gear in main-hub rooms only |
| Ignoring partner health | One death resets your whole run | Check both health bars every few rooms |
| Saving in one slot | No way to recover from a bad state | Rotate at least two save slots |
| Skipping files on the first run | Forces a second full playthrough | Read everything the first time |

A quick word on challenge runs: attempt them only after you've unlocked bonus weapons and memorized the map. Community reports are consistent that restricted runs on a fresh save are the number-one source of abandoned 100% attempts.

## FAQ

**Is this Resident Evil 0 achievement guide suitable for a first playthrough?**
Yes, but use it as a roadmap rather than a checklist. Play your first run casually to learn the map, then follow the step order above for the rest.

**How long does full completion take?**
It depends heavily on skill and how many runs you combine, but community reports generally place a full completion in the dozens of hours, with Leech Hunter taking a meaningful share of that.

**Do I need Leech Hunter for 100% completion?**
In almost every version, yes. Leech Hunter achievements are part of the main list, and its bonus weapon rewards make Hard mode far more manageable.

**What's the single most important tip in this Resident Evil 0 achievement guide?**
Plan your item drops. With no item boxes, inventory routing is the skill that separates a smooth run from a miserable one — and it affects every other achievement in the game.
