## Why Resident Evil 0 Items Work Differently

Resident Evil 0 items don't behave like items in any other classic Resident Evil game. There are no item boxes, no save-room stash, and no chest that magically teleports your gear across the map. Instead, everything you pick up lives in a small per-character inventory — and when you run out of room, you drop it on the floor and hope you remember where it landed. That single design choice makes Resident Evil 0 items the real antagonist of the game.

Why does it matter? Because most players who bounce off the game don't quit over difficulty. They quit because they left the wrong item three rooms back and now have to walk through a leech-infested hallway to get it. Understanding the system before you start is the difference between a smooth playthrough and a frustrating one.

| Rule | Earlier Resident Evil games | Resident Evil 0 |
|---|---|---|
| Item storage | Item boxes in save rooms | No boxes — drop items anywhere |
| Inventory capacity | Shared, expandable stash | Six slots per character |
| Retrieval | Pull from any box on the map | Walk back to where you dropped it |
| Party size | One playable character | Rebecca and Billy, switchable |
| Risk level | Low — stash is safe | High — items sit in hostile rooms |

The key mental shift is this: in Resident Evil 0, the floor *is* your item box. The game remembers every item you drop and where you dropped it, so nothing is ever lost permanently. That's the good news. The bad news is that the game also remembers that you left your Shotgun in a room that now has three zombies in it.

## The Core Item Categories in Resident Evil 0

Every item in the game falls into one of five buckets, and knowing which bucket you're dealing with tells you how much inventory space to spend on it.

| Category | Examples | Takes up space? | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Healing | Herbs, First Aid Spray | Yes | Rebecca can combine herbs; Billy cannot |
| Ammo | Handgun rounds, shells, grenade rounds | Yes | Stacks in one slot per type |
| Weapons | Handgun, Shotgun, Grenade Launcher, Magnum | Yes | Usually one or two slots each |
| Key items | Keys, emblems, tools, the Hookshot | Yes | Often bulky and used only once or twice |
| Documents | Files, notes, reports | Yes | Safe to read and drop immediately |

The practical takeaway: **documents are free**. Read a file, learn what it says, then dump it on the spot. There is no reason to carry a note across the mansion when it does nothing but occupy a slot you could give to a herb.

Key items are the opposite problem. They're mandatory, they're often large, and the game rarely tells you where they'll be needed next. Community reports consistently name the Hookshot as the single most annoying item in the game — a grappling tool you need to cross specific gaps, which then sits in your bag taking up space long after you've used it.

## Healing Items: Herbs, Sprays, and Who Can Mix Them

Healing is where the two-character system really shows its teeth. Rebecca is the team medic, and in Resident Evil 0 she is the one who can combine herbs into stronger mixtures. Billy can carry herbs and use them, but he can't mix them — so any herb-combining you want done has to happen while you're controlling Rebecca.

That's a small rule with big consequences. If you're deep in an area controlling Billy and your inventory is full of single green herbs, you're carrying dead weight. Hand them to Rebecca, mix them, and hand back a better item.

| Combination | Effect | Worth it? |
|---|---|---|
| Green Herb | Restores a small amount of health | Baseline |
| Green + Green | Restores more health than a single green | Yes, early game |
| Green + Red | Strongest standard heal | Yes, always |
| Green + Blue | Heals and cures poison | Situational |
| Green + Red + Blue | Full heal plus poison cure | Excellent |
| First Aid Spray | Full heal, no mixing required | Save for emergencies |

A few practical notes that experienced players repeat often:

- **Red herbs do nothing alone.** They only exist to amplify a green herb, so never carry a lone red herb into a fight.
- **Blue herbs matter more than they look.** Poison drains health steadily, and in some sections of the game you'll be dealing with venomous enemies.
- **First Aid Sprays are one-slot full heals.** They're the most efficient healing per inventory slot in the game, which makes them ideal for whoever is doing the fighting.
- **Split your healing between both characters.** If all your herbs are on one character and that character gets separated or pinned, you're in trouble.

## Weapons and Ammo: What to Carry and When

Weapons in Resident Evil 0 follow the classic escalation curve, but the tight inventory makes the choice of *what to bring* far more interesting than in other entries. You will almost never have room for everything.

| Weapon | Ammo Type | Best Used For | Carry Advice |
|---|---|---|---|
| Knife | None | Finishing downed enemies | Free, always keep it |
| Handgun | Handgun rounds | Standard enemies, ammo is common | Keep on both characters if you can |
| Shotgun | Shells | Crowded hallways, close-range burst | Carry when entering new areas |
| Grenade Launcher | Grenade rounds | Larger enemies, grouped targets | Boss and elite encounters |
| Magnum | Magnum rounds | High-damage boss damage | Ammo is scarce — save it |
| Submachine Gun | SMG rounds | Fast, sustained damage | Heavy ammo consumption |
| Rocket Launcher | Rockets | The finale | Situational |

The general strategy most players settle on: give one character the Handgun as a reliable fallback, and let the other carry the heavy weapon. Because you can switch characters mid-combat, you effectively get two loadouts at once — which is the closest thing Resident Evil 0 has to a difficulty relief valve.

Ammo discipline matters here more than in most Resident Evil games. Because you can't stash surplus shells in a box, every extra ammo type you're hoarding is a herb you aren't carrying. Drop the ammo you don't need in a room you'll pass through again, and pick it up when the situation calls for it.

## Key Items, the Hookshot, and Inventory Management Tips

Key items are the source of most inventory pain in Resident Evil 0 items management, because they're mandatory, often bulky, and sometimes needed in two different places. The Hookshot is the classic example — you need it to cross gaps in more than one area, which means it can't just be abandoned after its first use.

| Situation | Recommended Move |
|---|---|
| You just used a key item | Check whether it's needed again before dropping it |
| Both inventories are full | Drop documents and surplus ammo first |
| You're entering a new area | Leave heavy weapons near the entrance, not deep inside |
| You're about to fight a boss | Consolidate healing on the active fighter |
| You found a big new weapon | Drop the old one in a central, memorable room |

Some habits that pay off across a full playthrough:

- **Use landmarks, not memory.** Drop items in rooms with distinctive features so you can find them again quickly.
- **Treat the main hall or a central corridor as your bank.** Scattering items evenly across the map is how you end up doing laps.
- **Plan around the Hookshot.** Clear inventory space before you pick it up rather than after.
- **Remember that only Rebecca mixes herbs.** Route herb pickups through her when convenient.
- **Never drop an item in a room you can't re-enter.** Some areas close permanently behind you.

Player experience note: many veterans recommend doing a "dry run" of sorts — pick up everything early, then deliberately prune your inventory before each new zone. It feels wasteful the first time and saves hours by the third.

## Leech Hunter and Unlockable Rewards

Finishing the main campaign unlocks **Leech Hunter**, a bonus mode where you collect Leech Charms scattered across the map while dealing with enemies. You play as Rebecca or Billy and can carry over what you learned about inventory management — because the same tight slot limits apply.

Rewards scale with how thoroughly you collect. Grabbing every charm unlocks the best bonus gear, while partial runs still hand out useful unlocks. Alongside Leech Hunter, the modern HD Remaster of the game adds **Wesker Mode**, a bonus scenario where you play as Albert Wesker with his own distinct abilities — a fun reward for players who've already mastered the base game's item economy.

## FAQ

**Do items I drop in Resident Evil 0 ever disappear?**
No. Items you drop stay exactly where you left them for the rest of the game. That's what makes the drop-anywhere system workable — but it also means your dropped items stay in rooms that may still have enemies in them.

**Who should carry the healing items in Resident Evil 0?**
Split them. Rebecca should carry the herbs she needs to combine, and the character doing most of the fighting should carry at least one ready-to-use heal. Don't put every First Aid Spray on one person.

**Can Billy mix herbs in Resident Evil 0?**
No — only Rebecca can combine herbs into mixed versions. Billy can carry and use herbs, so pass them to Rebecca when you want them upgraded.

**What's the most annoying item in Resident Evil 0?**
Community consensus points to the Hookshot. It's a required key item that takes up serious inventory space and is needed in more than one location, forcing you to plan around it instead of dropping it for good.

**Do I need to keep documents?**
No. Read them, enjoy the lore, and drop them immediately. Files have no mechanical use and just consume inventory slots that are better spent on herbs and ammo.
