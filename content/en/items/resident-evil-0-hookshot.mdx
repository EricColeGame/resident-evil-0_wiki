Few things in survival horror have caused as much quiet frustration as the grappling gun in Resident Evil 0. The Resident Evil 0 Hookshot is a key item, not a weapon: it fires a hooked line across a chasm so you can cross, and it never damages a single enemy. And yet you cannot finish the campaign without it. Understanding where the Resident Evil 0 Hookshot appears, when the game expects you to be carrying it, and how to squeeze it into a small inventory is the difference between a clean run and an hour of backtracking through monster-infested hallways.

This guide covers what the item does, how the drop-and-swap inventory system changes the way you carry it, and the mistakes that trip up first-time players.

## What the Hookshot Actually Is

The Hookshot is a grappling gun — a launcher that fires a hooked line at a fixed anchor point. Stand at the right spot, equip it, and your character fires the line, catches the anchor, and pulls themselves across the gap. It is one of the purest "puzzle item" designs in the series: it exists to solve terrain, not combat.

A few traits matter before you pick it up:

- **It is a key item, not a weapon.** It deals zero damage and cannot be aimed at enemies.
- **It only works at designated anchor points.** You cannot free-aim it at an arbitrary wall or ceiling.
- **It is required for progression.** Certain crossings are impossible without it.
- **It occupies physical inventory space.** It is a bulky item, and space is the scarcest resource in Resident Evil 0.

## Where to Find It and When You'll Need It

The Hookshot is found inside the Training Facility, the second major area of the campaign after the Ecliptic Express sequence. You acquire it while exploring that facility, and the level design is fair about it: you generally see the uncrossable gap shortly before the game hands you the tool that solves it.

From that point forward, the Hookshot becomes part of your kit for the rest of the story. There are a small number of later points where a Hookshot line is the only route forward, and that is exactly why so many players get stuck. Dropping it in a random room "for later" and then needing it three areas later is one of the most common complaints in community discussions of this game.

Because Resident Evil 0 has no item box, the floor is your storage. Anything you drop stays in that room. That single design choice turns a simple traversal tool into an inventory-management problem.

| Situation | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| First encounter with a wide gap | You need the Hookshot in the active character's inventory |
| Revisiting an earlier area | Re-crossing usually requires the item again |
| Later story areas with a chasm | A second required use; do not assume it is obsolete |
| Partner left behind | Switch characters and repeat the crossing |
| Item dropped in a far room | You must physically walk back and retrieve it |

## How to Use the Hookshot: Step by Step

Using the Hookshot is simple once you know the pattern, but the game does not spell it out clearly. The basic sequence is always the same.

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | Make sure the Hookshot is in the inventory of the character you are currently controlling |
| 2 | Walk to the edge of the gap or the marked firing position |
| 3 | Equip the Hookshot from the inventory menu |
| 4 | Aim toward the anchor point — the game highlights the valid target |
| 5 | Fire the line; the character latches on and crosses automatically |
| 6 | Confirm your partner has followed, or switch to them and cross manually |

Two practical notes. First, if the anchor point is not highlighted, you are standing in the wrong place — shuffle a step or two along the ledge. Second, if your partner AI stalls at the edge, switch characters and cross with them directly. It is faster than waiting for the AI to sort itself out.

## Inventory Management: The Real Challenge

Resident Evil 0 gives each of the two playable characters a six-slot inventory. There are no item boxes, no shared storage, and no way to expand capacity. A two-slot Hookshot therefore consumes a third of one character's entire carrying capacity — permanently, if you decide to keep it on hand.

That trade-off is the heart of the Hookshot's reputation. It is not a hard item to use; it is a hard item to *carry*.

| Item | Approximate slots | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Hookshot | 2 | The item in question; bulky and situational |
| Handgun | 1 | Light, always worth carrying |
| Shotgun | 2 | Heavy, but essential for close-range threats |
| Herbs (each) | 1 | Mixing saves space, and only Rebecca can mix |
| First Aid Spray | 1 | Instant full heal, no mixing required |
| Ammo (each type) | 1 | Stacks are consumed as you fire |

**Rebecca vs. Billy: who should carry it?**

| Factor | Rebecca | Billy |
|---|---|---|
| Inventory slots | Six | Six |
| Herbal mixing | Yes — combines herbs efficiently | No |
| Durability in a fight | Lower | Higher |
| Typical role | Support, puzzle solving, healing | Frontline combat and heavy lifting |
| Common recommendation | Carry it if you control Billy more often | Carry it if Rebecca needs free slots for herbs |

Player experience generally favors giving the Hookshot to whichever character has a free two-slot block and is *not* the one you are actively using for combat. Just remember that you cannot hand it over instantly — you have to drop it and have the other character pick it up.

## Common Mistakes That Waste Hours

Most Hookshot problems are self-inflicted, and they are all avoidable.

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Better approach |
|---|---|---|
| Dropping it in a room you cannot easily revisit | Forces a long, dangerous backtrack | Note the room you leave it in |
| Treating it as a weapon | You waste time aiming it at enemies | Switch to a firearm instead |
| Filling every slot with guns and ammo | No room left for a two-slot item | Reserve a two-slot gap in one inventory |
| Leaving it with the partner who is not crossing | The crossing simply will not happen | Switch characters and re-equip |
| Assuming it becomes useless late in the game | You get stranded at a later chasm | Keep it until the campaign is over |
| Forgetting your partner at the edge | They can be left behind mid-crossing | Check the partner before moving on |

One more habit worth building: before you leave any room, scan your inventory and ask whether the Hookshot is where it needs to be. In a game with no item box, "where is my stuff?" is a real gameplay mechanic.

## Tips for Repeat Playthroughs and Speedruns

Once you know the campaign, the Hookshot shifts from a puzzle to a routing problem.

- **Plan the carry window.** Community speedrunners treat the Hookshot as a temporary passenger — pick it up, use it, and shed it as soon as the required crossing is done.
- **Combine the return trip.** If you must backtrack to retrieve it, do it while you are already heading that direction for another objective.
- **Keep herbs on Rebecca and guns on Billy.** This split leaves predictable free space for bulky items.
- **Practice the partner crossing.** A smooth character swap at the ledge saves several seconds per use.
- **Do not hoard.** Ammo you are not using is a slot you could be using for a key item.

## FAQ

**Can you use the Resident Evil 0 Hookshot as a weapon?**
No. It deals no damage and cannot be aimed at enemies. It exists purely for traversal at designated anchor points.

**Where do you find the Resident Evil 0 Hookshot?**
It is obtained inside the Training Facility, the second major area of the game after the train sequence. You will typically see the gap it solves shortly before you acquire it.

**How many inventory slots does the Hookshot take?**
Player reports consistently describe it as a two-slot item, out of a six-slot inventory per character — a significant chunk of your carrying capacity.

**Do you need the Resident Evil 0 Hookshot to finish the game?**
Yes. Several crossings in the campaign cannot be completed without it, so treat it as an essential key item and never leave it somewhere you cannot easily return to.
