Few survival horror games punish careless packing the way Resident Evil 0 does. The GameCube prequel — later remastered for modern platforms — threw out the item box that had defined the series and replaced it with something far stranger: a Resident Evil 0 inventory that lives in the rooms you leave behind. If you're playing it for the first time, the system feels like a trap waiting to spring. Once it clicks, it becomes the smartest puzzle in the game, and the reason veteran players still argue about it twenty years later.

## How the Resident Evil 0 Inventory System Works

Resident Evil 0 gives you two playable characters and splits your carrying capacity between them. Rebecca Chambers and Billy Coen each have six item slots, for twelve total. That sounds generous until you notice the catch: every single item — a shotgun, a key, a green herb, a box of ammo — eats exactly one slot. There is no size grid here, no Tetris-style packing like the 1996 original.

The bigger change is what happened to item boxes. Resident Evil 0 removed them from the mainline series for the first and only time. Instead, you can drop any item onto the floor of whatever room you're standing in, and it stays there for the rest of the run. Enemies don't steal it. It doesn't vanish after a cutscene. Your storage is the map itself.

When Rebecca and Billy are in the same room, you don't have to drop anything at all. Open the item screen and use the Exchange command to move gear directly between them — the fastest way to hand off a key item or pass ammo to whoever is doing the shooting.

| Feature | Classic Resident Evil (RE1/RE2 style) | Resident Evil 0 |
|---|---|---|
| Storage | Item boxes in save rooms | No item boxes at all |
| Capacity | Shared box, effectively unlimited | 6 slots per character, 12 total |
| Item size | Items occupy 1–2+ grid spaces | Every item occupies exactly one slot |
| Moving gear | Deposit and withdraw at a box | Drop on the floor anywhere |
| Character transfers | Not applicable (single hero) | Exchange when both are in one room |
| Tracking | Box contents list | Map flags rooms containing dropped items |
| Main risk | Forgetting what's in the box | Forgetting *where* you dropped something |

Everything else follows from those two rules. Because drops are permanent, smart players build caches — clusters of spare ammo, healing items, and puzzle tools parked in rooms they know they'll pass through again.

## Rebecca vs. Billy: Splitting the Resident Evil 0 Inventory

Who carries what matters more here than in any other entry in the series. Billy is the fighter: he absorbs more damage, and he's the one you want holding the shotgun when a Hunter shows up. Rebecca is the puzzle solver and the medic, and she goes down fast if she's left holding nothing but a key.

That gives you a clean division of labor, and community reports consistently land on the same split.

| Role | Rebecca | Billy |
|---|---|---|
| Best for | Healing items, key items, puzzle tools | Weapons, ammo, heavy firepower |
| Starting weapon | Handgun | Handgun |
| Durability | Low — protect her | High — he tanks hits |
| Suggested slots | 2 healing, 2 key items, 1 weapon, 1 open | 2 weapons, 3 ammo, 1 open |
| Why | She's usually the one interacting with terminals and devices | He's the one taking hits and clearing rooms |

The "one open slot" on each character is not a suggestion. Leaving zero flexibility means that the moment you find a key item, you're forced into a drop-and-fetch detour. One spare slot per character prevents most of that.

A good habit: when you clear a room and know you're coming back through it, dump everything you don't immediately need. Travel light, fight light.

## What to Carry and What to Drop

The single biggest mistake new players make is treating the Resident Evil 0 inventory like a backpack instead of a logistics network. You are not supposed to carry everything. You are supposed to carry the right things and park the rest.

| Item type | Carry or stash? | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Green, red, blue herbs | Carry, then mix immediately | A mixed herb compresses two or three slots into one |
| First Aid Spray | Stash in a cache | Full heal in one slot, but you rarely need it on hand |
| Handgun ammo | Carry on Billy | Most plentiful ammo type; keeps his weapon ready |
| Shotgun / grenade launcher | Carry one, stash the other | Heavy weapons eat slots you need for key items |
| Key items and tools | Carry until used, then drop | Many are one-time use — don't haul dead weight |
| Ink ribbons | Stash near a typewriter | Only needed at save points |
| Hookshot | Drop where you'll need it next | The classic slot-eater; it can lock up a slot for hours |

Herb mixing deserves its own paragraph. Two herbs cost two slots. Mix them into a single combined herb and they cost one. Do this the moment you have the components, not later — every minute you delay is a minute you're walking around with a wasted slot. Combining green and red gives you a stronger heal, and a three-herb mix handles both damage and poison in a single item.

## Common Resident Evil 0 Inventory Mistakes

Most of the frustration players report comes from four or five repeatable errors. All of them are fixable on a second run — or right now, if you're mid-playthrough.

| Mistake | What it costs you | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Leaving gear on the Ecliptic Express | Those items are gone permanently | Consolidate before the train sequence ends; the train is a one-way trip |
| Hauling the Hookshot everywhere | One slot locked up for hours | Drop it at the last spot you used it and collect it on the way back |
| Hoarding ammo "for the final boss" | Wasted slots, and harder mid-game fights | Use what you find; handgun ammo is common enough |
| Loading everything onto one character | The other has no healing when you need it | Split healing items across both |
| Never using Exchange | Endless drop-and-pickup loops | Swap directly whenever both characters share a room |
| Dropping items in a hurry | You forget where they are | Use the map's dropped-item markers before you move on |

The train warning is the one that ends runs. Once you leave that opening area, there is no route back, and anything sitting on the floor of a train car stays there forever. Sort your gear before you step off.

## Advanced Tips for Resident Evil 0 Inventory Management

Once the basics are automatic, the Resident Evil 0 inventory becomes a tool for routing rather than a source of anxiety. These approaches come from experienced players and speedrunners.

| Scenario | Inventory approach | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Normal story run | Two mobile kits plus three or four caches | Cache near save rooms and main halls |
| Boss encounters | Pre-stage ammo and healing in the room before | Avoid mid-fight menu panic |
| Leech Hunter | Treat every slot as precious | The minigame is a pure stress test of the drop-and-cache mindset |
| Wesker Mode | Lean on Wesker's mobility | He replaces Billy and changes how aggressive you can be |
| Speedruns | Script every drop | Runners plan pickups down to the individual room |

Two quality-of-life details help a lot. First, the item screen shows both characters' slots side by side, so you can plan a handoff before you even enter the room. Second, dropped items are marked on the map, which turns backtracking from guesswork into a checklist.

## FAQ

**Does Resident Evil 0 have an item box?**
No. Resident Evil 0 is the only mainline entry that removed item boxes entirely. Storage is handled by dropping items on the floor, where they remain for the rest of the game.

**How many slots does each character get?**
Rebecca and Billy each carry six items, for twelve total across the team. Every item takes exactly one slot regardless of type or size.

**Can you lose items you've dropped?**
Only in areas you can't return to, most notably the opening Ecliptic Express sequence. Everywhere else, dropped gear stays exactly where you left it.

**Can Rebecca and Billy trade items directly?**
Yes. When both characters are in the same room, open the item screen and use Exchange to move gear between them instantly — no dropping required. Mastering this one command is what separates a smooth Resident Evil 0 inventory from a constant backtracking slog.
